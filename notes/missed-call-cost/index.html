What a missed call actually costs an Australian service business

Most missed calls don't come back — they go to the next number in the search results. Here's the four-number payback math, and how to tell whether the leak is big enough to fix.

Johan Iskandar · 27 July 2026

A missed call at an Australian service business typically costs the value of a job, because most callers don't leave a voicemail — they call the next business on the list. If your average job is worth A$300 and you miss five callable enquiries a week, that's roughly A$78,000 a year walking out the door before you count referrals and repeat work (illustrative market math — plug in your own numbers below).

That's the payback case for answering every call, and it's why a voice agent is one of the fastest-return automations for trades, clinics, and home services. Not because AI is exciting — because the leak is large, invisible on your P&L, and fixable.

The math, step by step

Four numbers turn "we miss a few calls" into a figure you can act on:

  • Missed calls per week — check your phone logs, not your memory. Most owners underestimate this by half.
  • Share that were real enquiries — strip out spam and existing customers; call it 60–70% for most service businesses.
  • Your close rate on a live enquiry — the ones you do answer, how many book?
  • Average job value — first job only, to stay conservative.

Multiply them out and annualise: missed × enquiry-share × close-rate × job-value × 52. The number is almost always bigger than the entire cost of fixing it. This is the same pain × volume ranking from the admin-work triage method — missed calls just tend to win it outright for service businesses.

Why voicemail isn't the safety net you think

The assumption that a missed call "comes back" is where the money hides. Modern callers treat voicemail as a dead end: research and everyday behaviour both point the same way — most people hang up on a machine and dial the next result. After hours is worse, and after-hours is exactly when a lot of service enquiries happen. A call that goes unanswered at 7pm on a Sunday isn't deferred; it's gone.

What closing the leak actually costs

An AI receptionist or voice agent answers on the first ring, at any hour, captures the job, and books or routes it — for a fixed build plus roughly A$50–300 a month to run (market figures). Set that against the annual figure you just calculated. For most service businesses the running cost is a rounding error next to a single month's recovered jobs; our full structure, quoted before any build, is on the pricing page.

This isn't universal. If you miss two calls a month, don't build anything — the leak is too small to bother. The math is the filter: it tells trades, clinics, and solar and home-service businesses with real call volume whether the phone is quietly costing them a hire's worth of revenue, or whether they're fine as they are. Run your own numbers first; build only if they're ugly.
