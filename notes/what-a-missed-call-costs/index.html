What a missed call costs an Australian service business (the payback math)

The most expensive calls are the ones you never knew you missed. The math on what unanswered enquiries cost an AU service business — and where an AI voice agent changes it.

Johan Iskandar · 20 July 2026

A missed call at an Australian service business costs you the whole job, not just the call. Market framing, not a performance claim: if your average job is worth A$500 and you miss two callable enquiries a week, that's roughly A$52,000 a year of work walking to whoever answered instead (run it on your own job value — the figure moves with your numbers). This note does the payback math and shows where an AI voice agent changes it.

The reason missed calls are so expensive is that they're invisible. A missed call leaves no invoice, no angry email, no line in the P&L — the customer simply rings the next business on the search results and books there. You never find out about the job you didn't win.

The payback math, step by step

Four numbers decide it. Missed callable calls per week — the enquiries that go unanswered during jobs, at lunch, or after hours. Average job value. Conversion rate on answered enquiries. And the recovery rate — the share of currently-missed calls a voice agent would answer and book. Miss 5 calls a week, book 40% of them at a A$500 job value, and that's roughly A$1,000 a week — about A$52,000 a year — recovered from calls you're already paying marketing to generate.

Why the calls get missed in the first place

It's rarely negligence. A two-person trade can't answer while both are on the tools; a clinic's front desk is with a patient; the plumber's best leads come at 6pm when the office is closed. The calls aren't missed because nobody cares — they're missed because a human can only be in one place, and demand doesn't wait. That's exactly the high-volume, always-on, rule-bound work an agent is built for.

Where an AI voice agent changes the number

An AI voice agent for business answers the calls a person can't get to: it picks up on the second ring, captures the job details, books the callback or the appointment, and logs the lead in your CRM. The enquiry that used to hit voicemail becomes a booked job. This is one of the fastest-payback workflows for AU SMBs precisely because you've already spent the marketing money to make the phone ring — the agent just stops the leaks. The full build is covered in what an AI receptionist is and costs, and the broader hire-versus-build trade-off in the cost comparison against hiring.

Field example: solar and trades installers live on inbound phone leads with high job values, so a single recovered booking can cover a month of running cost — see the solar write-up for how the intake flow fits that model.

Run your own number before you buy anything

Don't take a generic figure — use yours. Multiply your missed callable calls, your job value, your conversion, and a conservative recovery rate. If the annual number dwarfs a fixed build plus roughly A$50–300 a month in running costs (market figures; your spec carries your number), the payback is real; if it doesn't, a voice agent isn't your priority. A Foundations Session runs that math on your actual call data, in writing, before you commit — and if the numbers don't work, we say so and stop there.
