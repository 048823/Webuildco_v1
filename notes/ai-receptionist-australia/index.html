AI receptionist for Australian SMBs: what it is, what it costs

A voice agent that answers every call, books the routine ones, and hands off the rest — for a fixed build plus roughly A$50–300 a month to run. What it does, and how to compare the cost honestly.

Johan Iskandar · 27 July 2026

An AI receptionist is a voice and messaging agent that answers your business calls, handles the routine ones — hours, availability, booking, basic questions — and captures and routes the rest to a human, 24/7. For most Australian SMBs it costs a fixed one-time build plus roughly A$50–300 a month to run (market figures — your spec carries your number), versus the A$65,000–85,000 a year a full-time front-desk hire costs loaded (market figures; check current award rates). It isn't a robot pretending to be a person — it's a system that stops calls going to voicemail.

The reason this workflow tops so many shortlists is blunt: a missed call at a service business is usually a lost job, and it goes to the next number on the search results. An AI receptionist exists to make sure the phone is always answered — including at 9pm and on the weekend, when a real receptionist isn't there.

What an AI receptionist actually does

  • Answers every call — first ring, after hours, or when the line's already busy and the second caller would otherwise hit voicemail.
  • Handles the routine — opening hours, location, service area, availability, standard FAQs, and simple bookings straight into your calendar.
  • Captures the lead — name, number, job details, urgency — and logs it in your CRM instead of on a sticky note.
  • Escalates by design — anything outside its rules gets taken as a message and routed to the right person, with the full call context attached.

What it deliberately doesn't do is fake judgment. A good deployment runs supervised until proven: it books and answers what it's sure about, and hands off the rest rather than guessing. That's the difference between a receptionist agent and the frustrating phone tree everyone hangs up on.

What it costs — and what to compare it against

There are three cost shapes on the market. Per-minute voice platforms bill for talk time and suit low, spiky call volume. Monthly SaaS receptionists charge a flat subscription but you're renting — the config and the number of your calls live in their account. An owned agent is built once, runs in your own accounts, and costs a fixed quote plus the A$50–300/month running range above. The honest comparison isn't agent-vs-nothing; it's the full running cost of an owned agent against a receptionist wage, an answering service, or the revenue currently leaking out through voicemail.

Our own pricing follows the owned model — a fixed written quote after scoping, running costs stated up front, no lock-in — and it's all on the pricing page. What you should refuse from any provider is a number before they've seen your call volume; that's a guess dressed as a quote.

Is an AI receptionist right for your business?

It pays back fastest when calls are both frequent and revenue-linked: trades, clinics, home services, real estate, anyone where a booked call is a booked job. If your call volume is low or every call genuinely needs a human, a person or a hybrid is the better answer — the point of the missed-call payback math is to check whether the leak is big enough to fix before you build anything.
