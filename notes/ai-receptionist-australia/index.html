AI receptionist for Australian SMBs: what it is, and what it costs

Answers every call, books the job, logs the lead — around the clock. What an AI receptionist actually does, what it costs in AUD, and where a human still wins.

Johan Iskandar · 20 July 2026

An AI receptionist is a voice (and often chat) agent that answers your business calls, captures the caller's details, books appointments, and logs the lead in your system — 24/7, including the calls that arrive after hours. For most Australian single-workflow builds it costs a fixed one-time build price plus roughly A$50–300 a month to run (market figures; your spec carries your number).

It is not a voicemail with a friendlier greeting, and it is not a phone tree. A voicemail captures a message; an AI receptionist handles the call — understands what the caller wants, answers routine questions, books the job, and writes it into your calendar and CRM before the caller has hung up.

What an AI receptionist actually does on a call

A working build does four things end to end: it answers every call (missed, overflow, or after-hours), understands the request in natural speech, acts by booking an appointment or capturing a qualified lead, and logs the whole interaction where your team can see it. If it can't do all four, it's a menu, not a receptionist — the same read/decide/act/log test that defines any real AI agent.

What it costs in Australia

Pricing has two parts. The build is a fixed quote agreed after scoping — a single-workflow voice agent typically goes live in 3–4 weeks. The running cost is model usage plus telephony and any tool subscriptions: roughly A$50–300 a month for most single-workflow builds (market figures for typical usage, not a guarantee). The structure — scope first, fixed price in writing before any build — is on the pricing page. There's no per-seat licence and no lock-in: you own the system.

Why the payback is fast for service businesses

A human receptionist costs a loaded salary and works 38 hours a week; the calls that convert best often arrive outside those hours. An AI receptionist answers the 7pm enquiry from a customer who found you on Google and would otherwise have called the next name on the list. For a business where the average job is worth hundreds of dollars, catching even a handful of otherwise-missed calls a week pays the running cost many times over — the missed-call payback math runs the numbers. Front-desk-heavy fields feel this most; intake-driven practices such as legal firms are a common fit, where first-touch speed decides who books.

The honest limits

An AI receptionist is not a full human replacement and shouldn't be sold as one. It handles the high-volume, rule-bound slice — answering, qualifying, booking, logging — and routes anything ambiguous or sensitive to a person by design. It should run supervised at first: drafting and booking under human review until the logs prove it, then earning autonomy category by category. Ask any provider to show you the approval gates and the audit log before you sign.

The short version: an AI receptionist answers, qualifies, books, and logs your calls around the clock for a fixed build plus roughly A$50–300 a month to run. A Foundations Session prices it against your actual call volume, in writing, before you commit.
