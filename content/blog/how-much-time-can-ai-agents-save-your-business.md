Every owner we talk to asks the same question first: *"What would this actually save me?"* Not in vague productivity terms — in hours and dollars.

Here's the honest way to work it out, using the same method we use when scoping a build.

## Start with the repetitive hours, not the technology

Ignore AI for a minute. List the tasks in your business that are:

- **High volume** — they happen daily or weekly, not once a quarter
- **Rule-based** — a competent new hire could do them with a checklist
- **Interruption-driven** — phone calls, inbound emails, form submissions

For a typical Australian SMB we audit, three buckets dominate:

1. **Answering and triaging calls** — reception, missed-call callbacks, booking changes
2. **Lead follow-up** — qualifying enquiries, chasing quotes, updating the CRM
3. **Document admin** — quotes, invoices, compliance paperwork, data re-entry

Most owners underestimate these. When we actually measure, the range is **15–40 hours a week** across a 5–20 person business.

## The maths is simple

Hours saved per week × fully-loaded hourly cost × 52 = annual saving.

An office admin at $38/hour handling 20 automatable hours a week is roughly **$39,500 a year**. Against a typical focused agent build plus $50–$300/month in running costs, payback usually lands in **2–5 months**. After that it compounds — the agent doesn't resign, take leave, or drop the phone at 5:01pm.

## What doesn't count

Be sceptical of ROI claims that include "faster decisions" or "better morale" as dollar figures. Real savings come from three measurable places:

- **Labour hours redirected** — the big one, calculated above
- **Revenue recovered** — missed calls and slow lead follow-up are lost jobs; answering every call is often worth more than the labour saving
- **Error reduction** — re-work and compliance mistakes have a real cost, but only count them if you can point at incidents

## Where the estimate goes wrong

Two traps we see:

**Overestimating coverage.** An agent handles the routine 80% of a workflow. The exceptions still need a human — that's by design, with guardrails and human approval on high-risk actions. Budget for 60–80% of the listed hours, not 100%.

**Underestimating volume.** Owners count the tasks they see, not the interruptions that never make it to a timesheet. If anything, measured volume comes in higher than the gut estimate.
