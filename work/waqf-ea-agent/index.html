Case study · Live pilot

An EA agent for a Sydney waqf institution

A knowledge-base-backed executive assistant agent, delivered over Telegram, rolled out in supervised phases. The client is anonymised until they approve being named — the build is real.

TelegramZohoKnowledge baseIslamic charity

The problem

A waqf (Islamic endowment) institution runs on a small team with a large surface area: donor enquiries, board coordination, program logistics, document trails. Institutional knowledge lived in people's heads and inboxes. Leadership time went to fielding the same questions and chasing the same threads — classic executive-assistant work, with no budget line for an executive assistant.

What we built

First, the knowledge base: organisational documents, processes, and FAQs structured so software can read them. Then the agent on top — reachable in Telegram, where the team already lives. It answers internal questions from the knowledge base, drafts responses and documents, and connects to Zoho for the operational records.

Delivery is phased, per the WeBuild Method: the agent started draft-only, with a human approving every output. Autonomy is being granted category by category as it proves itself — routine knowledge answers first, anything novel still goes through a human.

Why it's shaped this way

A charity handling donor relationships can't tolerate a confident wrong answer. That's why the knowledge base came before the agent — grounding answers in the institution's own documents instead of the model's imagination — and why the rollout is phased rather than big-bang. The gates are the standard Cage configuration: communications draft-only until proven, sensitive actions always gated.

Where this stands — honestly

This is a live phased pilot, not a finished rollout. No performance numbers are published because the proving run isn't done — when it is, the results get filmed under the Founding Five program and published with the client's sign-off, or not at all.
